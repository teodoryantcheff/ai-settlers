# Adopt Tone.js while keeping economic notices independent

The design and six-ticket implementation breakdown were confirmed on 2026-10-15. The migration reduces custom audio machinery while preserving the current player experience, allowing small timbre differences. Tone.js will own synthesis, mixing, effects and musical scheduling; game code will retain the three themes, adaptive arrangements, cue selection, cooldowns and spatial placement. Keep local procedural sound without downloaded audio assets, permit native context access only where browser lifecycle handling requires it, and remove the old audio engine instead of maintaining a fallback.

Economic notices must belong to an independent presentation observer, with text delivery and optional audio as separate effects. The current audio factory owns their delivery even though notices work before audio startup and while muted. Separating that responsibility prevents an audio dependency, startup failure or retry from suppressing or duplicating production observations. Preserve notice timing, coalescing, evidence links, load resets and live state bindings; neither audio nor this observer may advance simulation or consume its RNG. This boundary retains the presentation contract of [ADR-0004](0004-full-map-and-game-windows.md) and keeps transient notices distinct from the durable economic history in [ADR-0003](0003-preserve-economic-observation-history.md).

Preserve the existing controls, preferences and playback lifecycle. Music and ambience use cosmetic real time, continue during ordinary simulation pause and modal display, and suspend while the page is hidden. New-colony and load operations retain the session's score position. Immediate effects must not inherit musical lookahead or quantization, and overdue music must not produce a catch-up burst. Keep current cue limits and independent world-cue positioning.

Audio failure must leave the game running, show one nonblocking notice and allow retry through the audio controls. Skip one-shot effects that occur while audio is starting or recovering; never delay gameplay or replay a queue of missed sounds. Start music and ambience once audio is ready. Startup and recovery must avoid duplicated nodes, schedules or subscriptions and must clean up partial initialization.

Validation requires the repository's `npm test`, browser checks of startup, controls, background/resume behavior and representative sounds, and the user's listening check for recognizable sound. Preserve tests built through the real subsystem graph and typed internals hooks, while checking actual Tone behavior in the browser. Regenerate bundles and tests/game.html with `npm run build`, and update SPEC.md to describe the resulting ownership when the implementation changes it.

Implementation starts with [independent economic notices (#1)](https://github.com/teodoryantcheff/ai-settlers/issues/1), followed by [the shared Tone runtime and interface sounds (#2)](https://github.com/teodoryantcheff/ai-settlers/issues/2). [World effects (#3)](https://github.com/teodoryantcheff/ai-settlers/issues/3), [ambience (#4)](https://github.com/teodoryantcheff/ai-settlers/issues/4) and [music (#5)](https://github.com/teodoryantcheff/ai-settlers/issues/5) can then migrate independently. A temporary shared-context bridge keeps unmigrated categories working between slices; [final integration and acceptance (#6)](https://github.com/teodoryantcheff/ai-settlers/issues/6) removes it after all three migrations. Native issue dependencies record these blocking edges.


## Implementation and acceptance

The completed runtime uses the pinned Tone.js 15.1.22 class entrypoint and one owned browser context, with separate interface, world-effect, ambience and score modules. The temporary bridge and raw synthesis have been removed. The independent economic-notice observer commits visible text and evidence before attempting optional audio. [SPEC.md](../../SPEC.md) records current ownership; [Tone audio acceptance](../tone-audio-acceptance.md) records validation and remaining human acceptance.

On 2026-10-15 the user first requested reducing the loudness and sharpness of dings and pings. After the interface, world and score accents were softened, the user listened to the revised cue and Meadow extracts and confirmed: “Accept the revised sound.” The [acceptance record](../tone-audio-acceptance.md) links those accepted clips. Automated signal and lifecycle checks remain technical evidence rather than a substitute for that listening acceptance.
